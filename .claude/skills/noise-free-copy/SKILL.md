# Noise-free copy

Two kinds of noise weaken copy. Edit for both, in this order.

## 1. Theme noise

A block of copy carries more than one theme. A block is anything read as a unit: an eyebrow + headline + subline, a card, a section, a tagline. Every extra theme dilutes the strongest one, and readers keep one idea per block.

**Example**

- Noisy: eyebrow "Control room for AI agents" above the subline "Build apps and games with AI, and ship work you're proud to put your name on." Two themes: what the product is, and what you achieve.
- One theme: "Build apps and games with AI. Ship work you're proud to put your name on."

**Find it**

1. Name each block's theme in 2–4 words. If the name needs "and", the block has two themes.
2. Check neighbours. Eyebrow, heading, body and visual caption must share the theme.
3. Metaphors and labels count. A "control room" metaphor next to a "build with AI" claim is a second theme.
4. Taglines carry exactly one theme.

**Fix it**

- Keep the theme that carries the reader's benefit. Cut the others, or move them to the section where they lead.
- Don't merge two themes into one compound sentence. That is still two themes.

## 2. Mental noise

Words the reader must process but gains nothing from. Each one costs attention.

**Example**

- Noisy: "You make every call that matters. Storvald asks the right questions before work starts, and brings you each decision, with a recommendation, while it runs."
- Clean: "Storvald asks the right questions before work starts, and brings you each decision, with recommendation, while it runs." The first sentence only announced what the second one shows.

**Common sources**

- The body restating its own heading (heading "Details matter", body "Your app keeps the small touches that make it yours").
- The same claim made in two places on the page.
- Lead-in or summary sentences that the next sentence already proves.
- Filler qualifiers: "every step of", "in plain words", "even ones that".
- Contrast tails that add nothing: "…before it lands, not after it spreads".
- Passive voice, which makes the reader work out who acts. "Slop gets caught" → "Catch slop".

**Fix it**

Delete first, rephrase second. If a sentence or phrase can go without losing a fact, a benefit or the voice, it goes.

## Process

1. **Inventory.** List every block of copy in reading order.
2. **Theme pass.** One theme per block, and adjacent blocks don't compete.
3. **Mental-noise pass.** For each sentence ask: what does the reader gain? Remove restatements, filler and lead-ins. Turn passive into active.
4. **Page-level repetition.** If the same claim appears in two sections, keep it only where it leads.
5. **Respect the author's own lines.** If the author wrote or approved a line word for word, don't rewrite it. Flag the issue instead.
6. **Report.** Show before → after for each change, tagged [theme], [mental], [passive] or [repeat], with a few words on what was cut.

## Guardrails

- Never cut facts the reader needs: numbers, conditions, platforms, prices.
- Never drop the benefit to make a line shorter.
- Shorter is not the goal. One clear theme with zero waste is.
- Keep deliberate personality: a playful tagline or brand line is not noise.
